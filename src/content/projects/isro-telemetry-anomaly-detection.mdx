## The problem

Real-time telemetry anomaly detection has a narrow margin for error in both directions: miss
a real anomaly and a fault goes undetected; flag too many false positives and operators start
ignoring the system entirely. Classical single-method approaches (a threshold rule, a single
statistical test) tend to catch one anomaly *shape* well and miss others — telemetry drift
looks nothing like a sudden spike, and a single detector rarely handles both.

**Constraints:**
- Detection needed to run in real time against streaming telemetry, not as an offline batch
  analysis.
- The system needed to generalize across different anomaly shapes (gradual drift, sudden
  spikes, structural regime changes) without hand-tuning a separate detector for each.

As lead engineer, rather than picking one detection method, I designed the system to
ensemble four: rolling-mean and EMA
signals to catch gradual drift, an autoencoder to catch anomalies via reconstruction error,
and a Robust Random Cut Forest to catch structural outliers a purely statistical method would
miss. Combining outputs across all four gave broader anomaly-shape coverage than any single
method running alone.

## Key decisions

- **Ensemble over a single best-performing model.** Individually, each of the four methods
  had blind spots against certain anomaly shapes. Ensembling traded a small amount of
  simplicity for meaningfully broader coverage — worth it for a system where a missed anomaly
  has real operational cost.
- **Classical signals alongside a learned model.** Pairing simple, interpretable
  signals (rolling mean, EMA) with a learned autoencoder meant the system didn't depend
  entirely on a black-box model's behavior being well-calibrated on telemetry patterns it
  hadn't seen during training.

## Outcome

The ensemble reached 95% detection accuracy with 30% faster response times than the prior
approach, and the results were strong enough to secure a ₹1,800,000 ISRO "Respond Basket"
development grant to continue the work.
